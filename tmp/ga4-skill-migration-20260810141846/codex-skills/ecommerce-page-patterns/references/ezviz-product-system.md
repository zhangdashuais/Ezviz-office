# EZVIZ International Product System

Use this reference as the default base for EZVIZ-inspired smart-home product detail pages. It is a design system, not a requirement to copy a source page.

## Shared Visual System

- Build a long, full-width editorial product story rather than a card-heavy storefront.
- Use white and soft-gray bands for daytime/product proof. Reserve charcoal or black media bands for night vision, privacy, and security moments.
- Pair large plain-language headlines with concise evidence. Make the product, home scene, or app state the main visual signal in every section.
- Use clean sans-serif typography, strong but not theatrical display headings, dark neutral body text, restrained accent color, and ample vertical spacing.
- Use real product photography, home scenarios, app UI, installation diagrams, and coverage overlays. Avoid decorative gradients, stock-like abstraction, or repeated feature icons without proof.
- Keep cards flat and functional. Use them for a proof strip, setup steps, comparison choices, or repeatable device modules, not for every page section.

## Page Skeleton

1. **Hero**: product name, one outcome-led promise, product or lifestyle visual, 4-8 grouped proof points, and the region-appropriate CTA.
2. **Quick proof strip**: the 3-5 differentiators that make this model worth considering.
3. **Signature capability**: prove the key reason to buy with a focused visual module.
4. **Smart response**: show the detection, alert, control, or automation loop through an app UI or simple flow.
5. **Practical adoption**: cover installation, power, network, weather resistance, and included accessories.
6. **Trust**: cover privacy, local/cloud storage, safety, warranties, and limitations.
7. **Compatibility and choice**: add fit checks, accessories, or model comparison when purchase risk is material.
8. **Specification and support**: provide scannable specs, in-box content, footnotes, FAQ, manuals, and support links.

## Interaction Rules

- Use a sticky subnav for long pages: Overview, Features, Installation, Specs, Support. Include Accessories or FAQ when needed.
- Use a sticky purchase or availability bar only after the initial hero; label it according to market reality, such as Buy Now, Where to Buy, or Check Compatibility.
- Use before/after sliders for visual changes: night vision, WDR, distortion correction, or zoom. Do not make them decorative.
- Use tabs for mutually exclusive app states, unlock methods, or connection choices. Use accordions for dense specifications, FAQ, and mobile-only long content.
- Use a three-step installation module: Connect, Mount, Configure/Recharge. Add constraints and in-box accessories directly below the steps.
- Make product comparison task-led: "Which entry setup fits my door?" or "Which outdoor camera fits my network?" Do not compare arbitrary specs only.

## Copy And Disclosure

- Write headlines as outcomes; position metrics in a short proof line.
- Group feature strips into buyer-relevant themes, such as See Clearly, Respond Safely, Install Easily, and Store with Control.
- Explain connected-device value as a cause-and-effect chain, for example: door opens -> camera records -> app creates an event.
- Place purchase-critical qualifiers close to their claims: 2.4 GHz-only, gateway required, cloud subscription, regional availability, battery test conditions, compatible door thickness, lock body, or accessory sold separately.
- Use footnotes for testing context and legal claims. Do not rely on footnotes to disclose a capability dependency the buyer must know before checkout.

## Review Checklist

- Can a visitor understand the product's main use case and its strongest reason to buy in the first viewport?
- Does each major feature have visual proof instead of another generic icon row?
- Is the category-specific purchase risk explicit: door fit, wiring, network, power, gateway, subscription, or storage?
- Does the page show the app or device ecosystem when connected control is a stated benefit?
- Are night, privacy, and storage claims shown in their own evidence-rich modules?
- Do desktop and mobile preserve hierarchy, legibility, CTA access, and non-cropped product imagery?
